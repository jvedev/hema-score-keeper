## UX and visual review (2026-11-20, on the dev stack)

Looked at the Events list, Event overview and the tournament workspace (Overview and Stages tabs) in
the browser. Findings, most important first:

1. **Two visual languages.** The Competition screens (now hosted in Event-admin) use the shared
   `--hema-*` tokens (`packages/ui/src/styles/tokens.css`: plate/raised/rule surfaces, brass and
   verdigris accents, Big Shoulders Display headings, Atkinson Hyperlegible body). The Event-admin
   pages hard-code their own colors (`#101721`, `#e8edf5`, generic blue `#2563eb`) and use the system
   font. Moving between a tournament and a competition of the same Event feels like two products.
2. **Broken or weak outlines.** Panels sit inside panels inside panels (shell, tournament card, tab
   panel, stage list card, detail pane, sub-tabs), each with its own border and radius; the Stages
   panel has its own inner scrollbar; the stage card squeezes Edit/Delete next to the name. The
   Events list still shows dark-on-dark titles and white cards (the theme bug already fixed on the
   Event overview).
3. **Too many navigation levels.** Tournament tabs, then a stage list, then six stage sub-tabs
   (Overview, Arenas, Officials, Rounds, Matches, Pool-results). A stage's real content (its pools,
   bracket and matches per ring) is split over four sub-tabs, and the stage order (pools, elimination,
   semi-finals, final) is not visible anywhere.
4. **System words instead of user words.** "SQLite", "Database-mode", "POOL", "(POOL)", raw arena ids
   (UUIDs) in the match table, round "0", "1 1 stage", "1 rounds", "Pool-results"; a Dutch empty
   message ("Geen stage geselecteerd") in the English UI.
5. **Dead or vague actions.** "Edit tournament" and "New stage" do nothing; the Overview cards
   ("Schedule readiness: SQLite", "Live scoring: Ready") do not say what to do next.

**Direction (keeps the product's existing identity, no new brand):**
- One look across Event-admin: all Event-admin components use the shared `--hema-*` tokens and the
  competition apps' type pairing (Big Shoulders Display for titles and numbers, Atkinson
  Hyperlegible for text, IBM Plex Mono for scores and times). Fonts fall back to system fonts when
  the venue has no internet.
- Clean outlines: one surface level per region (page, one panel, rows), 1px `--hema-rule` borders,
  one radius, an 8px spacing grid, aligned columns, no inner scroll areas, visible keyboard focus.
- **Signature element: the stage path.** The Stages tab opens with a horizontal path of the
  tournament's stages in order (Pools, Elimination, Semi-finals, Final), each showing its state
  (done / current / next) and fighter count, with "Next stage" on the path. Choosing a stage shows one
  page without sub-tabs: settings summary with Edit, then the pools (standings per pool) or the
  bracket (a real bracket tree), then the matches grouped per ring in their order.
- The Overview tab answers "what do I do next" (for example "Pools are not scheduled yet: open Event
  planning", "Pool stage complete: fill the elimination bracket", "Start live scoring").
- Plain, translated words everywhere; no ids or enum names on screen.

**Bracket decisions (user, 2026-11-20; also in the spec, section 11):**
- **Stage structure:** one bracket stage by default. A tournament has a Pools stage and one
  Elimination stage that holds the whole bracket; its rounds are the bracket rounds (for example
  quarter-finals, semi-finals, final and bronze). New tournaments no longer get a separate Semi-final
  stage.
- **Separate final stages (setting, user 2026-11-20):** optionally the bronze match and the final are
  their own stages, after the Elimination stage: Elimination (up to the semi-finals), then Bronze
  final, then Final. Reason: when an Event has several tournaments (for example "Women+ longsword" and
  "Open longsword"), their bronze finals and finals are often fought in their own time slots, for
  example together at the end of the day. Like other stages they are then placed in time slots in the
  Event planner. The setting is off by default (everything in the Elimination stage) and sits with
  the other bracket rules in the ruleset. Automatic advancing works across the stages: semi-final
  winners go to the Final stage's match, the losers to the Bronze final stage's match. With the
  bronze match switched off there is no Bronze final stage.
- **Seeding (pluggable rule):** default "by pool place": all pool winners first, then all second
  places, and so on; within the same place by the pool ranking rules (match points, then points
  scored, then fewest conceded). Seeding is a named rule chosen in the ruleset, implemented behind a
  small rule interface so other rules can be added or swapped easily.
- **Pairing (pluggable rule):** default "standard, pools apart": classic bracket order (1 v 8, 4 v 5,
  2 v 7, 3 v 6 for eight places) with byes for the top seeds when there are fewer fighters than
  places; when two fighters from the same pool would meet in the first round, swap with the nearest
  seed so they do not. Also a ruleset rule.
- **Bronze match:** on by default, can be switched off in the ruleset; the two semi-final losers
  fight for third place.
- **Advancing:** automatic. When a bracket match result is saved or corrected, the winner is placed
  in the next match (and semi-final losers in the bronze match). A correction re-places them while
  the next match has not started; otherwise it is refused with a clear message.

Consequences for Part C: tasks 10 to 13 implement these rules; the bracket rules live in the
ruleset definition (seeding rule, pairing rule, bronze match on/off) with a small rule registry;
creating a tournament makes Pools and Elimination stages (plus Bronze final and Final
stages when the separate-final-stages setting is on; turning the setting on or off adds or removes
those stages while they have no matches); the stage path shows them and the Event planner schedules
them like any other stage; automatic advancing hooks into every
place a match result is saved or corrected (completing a match and the live-result projection).
